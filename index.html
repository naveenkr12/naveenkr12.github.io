Research & Publications

Debt Overhang, Bank Credit, and the Real Economy in India: Evidence from the NPA Cycle, 2015–2025 +
Working paper · Aug 2026

Uses India’s 2015–2025 NPA cycle to study how banking distress affects the link between credit and real activity. In a quarterly panel of bank groups and production sectors, credit growth in manufacturing and construction was associated with lower investment during the stress period — consistent with debt overhang and ‘zombie’ evergreening rather than genuine new lending. After balance sheets were repaired post-2022, that negative relationship reversed. Credit growth is a poor signal of real activity when banks are impaired; consumption, by contrast, is driven mainly by permanent income.

Impact of Sectoral Credit Growth on Consumption and Capital Formation in India — A Time-Series and Panel Data Analysis Using Quarterly Data (2015–2025) +
Working paper · Jun 2026

Investigates the causal impact of sectoral bank credit growth on private consumption and capital formation in India from 2015 to 2025. Across ten sectors, credit transmission proves sector-specific rather than universal: positive credit-to-consumption linkages are confined to Electricity and Construction, while for investment, Agriculture and FIRE show clear bank-lending channels — and the 2015–2019 NPA crisis created a debt overhang that suppressed capital formation in Manufacturing and Construction.

Monetary Policy Transmission Mechanisms in India — Evidence from ARDL Time Series and Panel Data Analysis +
Working paper · May 2026

Investigates the transmission mechanisms of monetary policy in India from Q4 FY2014-15 to Q2 FY2025-26. Using ARDL time series and panel data models to examine the interest rate and credit channels, a 100-basis-point repo rate reduction is found to increase aggregate credit growth by ~2.36 percentage points. At the sectoral level, the lending rate (WALR) is the dominant transmission variable, and transmission speed and magnitude vary significantly across ten GVA sectors — with mining responding most rapidly.

Regulatory Policies and Sectoral Credit Deployment in India: A Panel Data Investigation +
Working paper · May 2026

Explores whether macro-prudential regulatory policies — changes in risk-weighted assets (RWA) and provisioning norms — constrain bank credit deployment across sectors in India from 2015 to 2025. In a balanced panel of three bank ownership groups, RWA growth and credit growth prove co-driven by common demand conditions rather than a regulatory cost channel. Private Banks show strong regulatory-credit sensitivity, while Public Sector Banks show no significant relationship due to directed-lending mandates — with implications for Indian macro-prudential policy.

Forecasting Digital Payments System Data in India for Nowcasting Macroeconomic Variables: A Machine Learning and Deep Learning Perspective +
Research paper · Mar 2023
Presented at The Indian Econometric Society (Jan 2023) and the Indian Association for Research in National Income & Wealth (Mar 2023)

Explores the use of high-frequency digital payment system data in India for nowcasting quarterly macroeconomic variables. Daily transaction data from RTGS, UPI, NEFT, IMPS, and card payments are first forecast using deep learning models — Prophet with XGBoost error correction, feed-forward autoregressive neural networks, and weighted ensembles — then used to nowcast GDP and PFCE via ARIMA and OLS frameworks, demonstrating the value of high-frequency payments data for real-time macroeconomic monitoring.

The Impact of COVID-19 on Digital Payment Habits of Indian Households +
Journal article · Bulletin of Monetary Economics and Banking · Mar 2022
Presented at Bank of Finland (Economics of Payment Systems–X, Oct 2021) and the BME&B International Conference, Indonesia (Sep 2021)

Studies the COVID-19 lockdown as a structural break in digital payment adoption in India, focusing on first-time users. Using a large-scale survey dataset, the paper analyzes the determinants of this shift beyond standard demographic factors, highlighting the critical role of awareness of digital payment modes, access to smartphones and debit cards, and pandemic-related welfare transfers in accelerating adoption — noting that individuals who had previously discontinued digital payments due to adverse experiences re-entered the digital ecosystem during the lockdown.
